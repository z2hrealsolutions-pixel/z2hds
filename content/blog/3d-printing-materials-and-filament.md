The material you print in decides how a part looks, how strong it is, how much heat it can take and whether it can bend. This guide covers the 3D printing materials and filaments we print in at Z2H Design Studio, and what each is for.

## What is 3D printing filament?

Filament is the raw material for FDM 3D printing. It is a plastic wire wound on a spool, usually 1 kg of plastic per spool.

- **Thickness.** The common sizes are **1.75 mm** and 2.85 mm. Most printers today use 1.75 mm.
- **Accuracy.** Good filament keeps the same thickness along the whole spool. Uneven filament gives uneven prints.
- **Moisture.** Many filaments slowly absorb water from the air. Damp filament prints with bubbles, strings and weak layers, so spools are kept sealed and dry. This matters in a humid country like Sri Lanka.

## The main 3D printing materials

| Material | Strength | Heat | Flexible | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLA+ | Good | Low | No | Gifts, statues, prototypes |
| PLA | Fair | Low | No | Models, decor, display pieces |
| PETG | Very good | Medium | Slightly | Functional parts, parts near water |
| ABS/ASA | Very good | High | No | Parts near heat; ASA for outdoor use |
| TPU | Tough | Medium | Yes | Grips, seals, cases |
| Lightweight PLA | Fair | Low | No | Large, light props and display pieces |

### PLA

The most widely used 3D printing material. It prints easily with sharp detail and a clean finish, and comes in the widest range of colours. It softens in strong heat, so it is not the best choice for a part left in a hot car.

### PLA+

A tougher PLA, less brittle than standard PLA, with the same easy printing and good detail. It is our everyday choice for gifts, statues and most prototypes.

### PLA variants

PLA also comes in special finishes and blends. Common ones include silk (shiny), matte, and filaments mixed with fine particles such as wood fibre. They are chosen mostly for looks. Ask us which variants we have in stock for your job.

### PETG

Tougher than PLA and better with moisture and some heat. It bends a little before it breaks, which makes it good for brackets, covers, holders and parts that sit near water.

### PETG variants

PETG also comes in blends and special finishes, for parts that need PETG's toughness with a different look or extra stiffness. Ask us what is in stock.

### ABS and ASA

Hard, sturdy plastics that handle heat better than PLA. ABS is a classic engineering plastic. ASA is similar but also stands up to sunlight, so it is the better choice for parts used outdoors. Both shrink as they cool and need careful printing to stay flat.

### TPU (flexible)

A rubbery, flexible filament. It bends and springs back, which makes it right for grips, seals, bumpers, phone and device cases, and parts that absorb shock.

### Lightweight PLA

A PLA that foams as it prints, so the part has a lighter body. It is a good fit for large display pieces and costume props that people need to carry.

## How to choose

Tell us three things and we will choose the material with you:

1. **What is the part for?** Display, daily use, or a working part under load.
2. **Where will it be used?** Indoors, outdoors, near heat or near water.
3. **Must it bend?** If yes, TPU is usually the answer.
