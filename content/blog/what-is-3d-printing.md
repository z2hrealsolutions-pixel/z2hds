3D printing has gone from a lab tool to something anyone can use. At Z2H Design Studio in Maharagama we print statues, gifts, prototypes and working parts every day. Here is how it works, in plain words.

## How 3D printing works

Every 3D print follows the same four steps.

1. **A 3D model.** It starts with a digital file of the object. The file can be drawn in CAD software, made from photos or a 3D scan, or downloaded.
2. **Slicing.** A program called a slicer cuts the model into hundreds or thousands of thin layers, often 0.1 to 0.3 mm each. It also plans the path the printer will follow.
3. **Printing.** The printer builds the object one layer at a time, from the bottom up. Each new layer sticks to the one below it.
4. **Finishing.** Supports are removed, and the part is cleaned. Some parts are sanded or painted.

## The most common type: FDM printing

The most common kind of 3D printing is FDM (fused deposition modelling). It is the kind we use. A spool of plastic filament is fed into a hot nozzle, melted, and laid down in thin lines, like a very precise hot glue gun. The plastic cools and hardens in a moment, and the next layer goes on top.

FDM can print in many plastics, such as PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA and flexible TPU. Printers with a filament changer can also print several colours in one part. Read more in our guide to [3D printing materials and filament](/blog/3d-printing-materials-and-filament).

Other kinds of 3D printing use liquid resin hardened by light, or fine powder fused by a laser. You can read about them in our guide to [3D printing machines](/blog/3d-printing-machines-explained).

## What is 3D printing used for?

- **Prototypes.** Testing a new product before paying for moulds or factory tooling.
- **Working parts.** Brackets, jigs, mounts, housings and spare parts that are hard to find.
- **Personal gifts.** Statues and busts made from photos, name signs and keepsakes.
- **Home and decor.** Vases, lamps, planters and organisers in your own size and colour.
- **Education.** Models for school and university projects.

## Why choose 3D printing?

- **No moulds.** You can make one piece, or a hundred, without tooling costs.
- **Any shape.** Hollow, curved and detailed shapes that are hard to make any other way.
- **Fast changes.** Change the file and print again. Each version can be ready in hours or days.
- **Made to measure.** Every piece can be a different size, colour or design.

## What are the limits?

- **Layer lines.** FDM parts show fine lines from each layer. Finishing can reduce them.
- **Strength by direction.** A part is usually a little weaker between layers than along them, so the way it is placed on the printer matters.
- **Size.** Each printer has a maximum size. Bigger parts can be printed in pieces and joined.
- **Large batches.** For many thousands of the same part, moulding can be cheaper per piece.
