People often ask us which 3D printing machine they should buy, or which machine is best for a job. This guide explains the main types in simple words.

## The main types of 3D printing machine

| Type | How it works | Good for | Things to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FDM** (filament) | Melts plastic filament and lays it down layer by layer | Prototypes, working parts, gifts, large parts, multi-colour prints | Shows fine layer lines; the widest choice of materials |
| **Resin** (SLA, MSLA, DLP) | Hardens liquid resin with light, one layer at a time | Very fine detail, such as jewellery models and miniatures | Parts need washing and curing; resin must be handled with care |
| **Powder** (SLS, MJF) | Fuses fine plastic powder with a laser or heat | Strong nylon parts with no supports | Large, costly industrial machines |
| **Metal** (DMLS, SLM) | Melts metal powder with a laser | Metal parts for industry and medicine | Very costly; mostly used by large factories |

For most everyday jobs, FDM gives the best balance of cost, strength, size and choice of material. That is why it is the type we use at Z2H Design Studio.

## The parts of an FDM 3D printer

- **Frame.** Holds everything steady. A stiff frame gives cleaner prints at higher speeds.
- **Extruder.** Grips the filament and pushes it forward at a steady rate.
- **Hot end and nozzle.** Melts the filament. Nozzles are usually 0.4 mm wide; smaller ones give finer detail.
- **Build plate (bed).** The flat surface the part is printed on. It is usually heated so the first layer sticks.
- **Motion system.** Motors and belts that move the nozzle and the bed with great accuracy.
- **Filament changer.** On multi-colour printers, a unit that swaps between several spools during a print.
- **Enclosure.** Some printers have a closed case that keeps the heat in. It helps with materials such as ABS and ASA.

## What the specifications mean

- **Build volume.** The biggest part the machine can print in one piece, given as width, depth and height in millimetres.
- **Layer height.** How thin each layer is, often from 0.08 to 0.3 mm. Thinner layers look smoother but take longer.
- **Speed.** How fast the nozzle moves. Faster is not always better: detail and strength still need care.
- **Materials.** Which filaments the machine can print. A heated bed and an enclosure widen the choice.
- **Colours.** Whether the machine can print more than one colour in a part.

## Should you buy a 3D printer or use a service?

**Buying makes sense** if you will print often, enjoy learning the machine, and have time to tune settings, fix failed prints and maintain the printer.

**A 3D printing service makes sense** if you need a part done right the first time, need materials or colours you do not have, need a part designed for you, or need many copies quickly. You also skip the cost of the machine, spare parts and filament stock.
